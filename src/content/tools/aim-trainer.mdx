<section id="what-is" class="info-section">
  <div class="container">
    <h2>What is an aim trainer?</h2>

      An aim trainer is a tool for practicing and measuring mouse aim: how fast and how precisely
      you can move your cursor to a target and click it. This one gives you 30 targets, one at a
      time. Hit them all as fast as you can, and your hits, misses, and time become one aim score.

      Aim is the core mechanical skill in shooters, and it responds well to focused practice. A few
      short runs a day sharpen your flicks faster than hours of matches, because you spend every
      second of a run aiming.

      Aim combines two things this site measures separately: how fast you react and how fast you
      click. Test the first with the <a href="/">reaction time test</a> and the second with the{' '}
      <a href="/cps">clicks per second test</a>.

  </div>
</section>

<section id="one-score" class="info-section alt-bg">
  <div class="container">
    <h2>One mode, one score</h2>

      Most aim trainers offer a menu of modes: flick shots, grid shot, spider shot, tracking. Each
      mode produces its own score on its own scale, so none of them tells you how good your aim is
      overall.

      This test takes the opposite approach. One standardized format, fixed for every run: 30
      targets, one on screen at a time, consistent target sizing, and spawn distances held within a
      set band so every run demands about the same amount of cursor travel. Because the task never
      changes, your score means the same thing tomorrow, and your trend graph tracks real
      improvement.

      The whole formula fits in one line:

    <div class="note">
      <strong>Score = targets per second × accuracy × 100.</strong> Hit 30 targets in 15 seconds at
      90% accuracy: 2.0 × 0.90 × 100 = 180. Spraying clicks costs you accuracy, and slow,
      cautious aim costs you speed. Clean, fast flicks win.
    </div>

  </div>
</section>

<section id="how-it-works" class="info-section">
  <div class="container">
    <h2>How this aim trainer works</h2>
    <ol class="steps-list section-content">
      <li>
        <strong>Pick your target</strong>

          Choose a target style (bullseye, dot, ring, or reticle) and a color. Both are cosmetic:
          every style has the same hitbox, so pick what your eyes track best. Your browser
          remembers the choice.

      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Hit the starting target</strong>

          Click the center target to begin. The clock starts on that click, so every run measures
          your first flick from the same known cursor position.

      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Hit 30 targets</strong>

          Targets appear one at a time at a controlled distance from the last one. Click each as
          fast as you can. Clicks that land inside the arena but off the target count as misses.

      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Read your score</strong>

          After the 30th hit, the test locks and shows your aim score, accuracy, average time per
          target, and a rank from Beginner to Elite. Beat your best to see the "New best!" flag.

      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Run it again</strong>

          Your best score, average, trend graph, achieved ranks, and run history are saved locally
          so you can watch your aim sharpen over days and weeks.

      </li>
    </ol>
    <div class="note">
      <strong>Note:</strong> The keyboard can start and step through a run as an accessibility
      path, but only a pointer measures aim. Compare mouse scores with mouse scores and touch with
      touch.
    </div>

  </div>
</section>

    <h2>What is a good aim score?</h2>

      A good score on this aim trainer is anything above 160. Most people land between 100 and 160
      on their first runs, which works out to roughly 600 to 700 milliseconds per target at 85%
      accuracy. Scores fall into five tiers:

        <span class="rank-range">300+</span>

      <div class="rank-row pro">

        <span class="rank-range">220–299</span>
        <span class="rank-desc">Fast, precise aim. Experienced FPS-player territory.</span>
      </div>
      <div class="rank-row advanced">

        <span class="rank-range">160–219</span>
        <span class="rank-desc">Above average. Quick flicks with few misses.</span>
      </div>
      <div class="rank-row intermediate">
        <span class="rank-name">Intermediate</span>
        <span class="rank-range">100–159</span>
        <span class="rank-desc">The typical range for most people.</span>

      <div class="rank-row beginner">
        <span class="rank-name">Beginner</span>
        <span class="rank-range">Under 100</span>
        <span class="rank-desc">Room to grow. Sensitivity tuning helps a lot here.</span>

      For a feel of the math: 500ms per target at 92% accuracy scores 184 (Advanced), 400ms at 95%
      scores 226 (Pro), and cracking 300 takes roughly 320ms per target at 97%. Speed without

      This test isn't a scientific measurement, but the format never changes, which makes it a
      reliable benchmark for your aim and a way to track improvement.

<section id="tips" class="info-section alt-bg">
  <div class="container">
    <h2>How to improve your aim</h2>
    <div class="tips-grid section-content">
      <div class="tip-card">
        <span class="tip-card-icon" aria-hidden="true">
          🖱️
        </span>
        <h3>Tune your sensitivity</h3>

          Overshooting targets means your sensitivity is too high; dragging to reach them means
          too low. Adjust in small steps and give each setting a few runs before judging it.

      </div>
      <div class="tip-card">
        <span class="tip-card-icon" aria-hidden="true">
          💪
        </span>
        <h3>Aim with your arm</h3>

          Use your arm for the big movement and your wrist and fingers for the final correction.
          Wrist-only aiming caps your range and tires faster.

      </div>
      <div class="tip-card">
        <span class="tip-card-icon" aria-hidden="true">
          🔥
        </span>
        <h3>Warm up first</h3>

          Do two or three throwaway runs before your real attempt. Cold hands flick slow, and a
          short warm-up lifts both your speed and your accuracy.

      </div>
      <div class="tip-card">
        <span class="tip-card-icon" aria-hidden="true">
          🧘
        </span>
        <h3>Stay relaxed</h3>

          A death grip on the mouse ruins fine control. Loosen your grip, drop your shoulder, and
          let the flick stop itself instead of forcing it.

      </div>
      <div class="tip-card">
        <span class="tip-card-icon" aria-hidden="true">
          🎯
        </span>
        <h3>Accuracy before speed</h3>

          Misses cost more than slow hits. Aim to hit every target at a comfortable pace first,
          then push the pace while holding your accuracy above 90%.

      </div>
      <div class="tip-card">
        <span class="tip-card-icon" aria-hidden="true">
          📅
        </span>
        <h3>Practice in short sessions</h3>

          Ten focused minutes a day beats an hour once a week. Aim is a motor skill; your gains
          come from frequent, rested repetitions, and they compound over weeks.

      </div>
    </div>

  </div>
</section>

<section id="factors" class="info-section">
  <div class="container">
    <h2>Factors that affect your aim score</h2>
    <div class="factors-list section-content">
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Mouse sensitivity & DPI</h3>

          The single biggest factor you control. High sensitivity produces fast, wild flicks;
          low sensitivity produces slow, exact ones. Your best score sits at the setting where you
          can stop on the target in one motion.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Hardware</h3>

          A mouse with a good sensor and high polling rate tracks fast flicks without skipping. A
          high-refresh monitor shows the target sooner and your cursor position more often, which
          tightens your corrections.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Screen and arena size</h3>

          Target sizes and spawn distances scale with the arena, so runs on the same screen are
          comparable. A larger screen means more physical mouse travel per target, which is why
          cross-device comparisons are rough for every aim trainer.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Technique</h3>

          Flicking (one fast motion plus a micro-correction) beats dragging the cursor. Good
          aimers also reset their eyes to the next likely spawn area instead of admiring the hit.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Fatigue & focus</h3>

          Aim degrades before you notice it. Tired eyes track worse and tired forearms overshoot.
          If your trend graph dips at the end of a session, that's your signal to stop.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Input device</h3>

          Mouse, touchpad, and touchscreen are different skills with different ceilings. Pick one
          and track it; switching devices mid-week makes your trend graph lie to you.

      </div>
    </div>

  </div>
</section>
