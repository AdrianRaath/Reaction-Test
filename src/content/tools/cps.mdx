faq:
  - question: 'What is a good clicks per second (CPS) score?'
    answer: "For a standard mouse click, most people average around 6 to 7 clicks per second over a 5-second test. Anything above 8 CPS is fast, and sustained scores of 10 or more put you in elite territory. Specialized techniques like jitter clicking or butterfly clicking can push scores well past 10, but they take practice and aren't necessary for normal use."
  - question: 'What is the average CPS?'
    answer: 'The average clicks per second for an adult using normal single-finger clicking is roughly 6 to 7 CPS in a short 5-second burst. Averages drop over longer durations as your hand fatigues, so a 60-second test will usually show a lower CPS than a 5-second one.'
  - question: 'What is jitter clicking and butterfly clicking?'
    answer: 'Jitter clicking is tensing your arm and hand to create a rapid vibration that clicks the mouse very fast, often 10 to 14 CPS. Butterfly clicking alternates two fingers on one mouse button to roughly double your click rate, sometimes 15 to 25 CPS. Both can strain your hand, and some games consider them cheating, so use them with care.'
  - question: 'Does my mouse affect my clicks per second?'
    answer: 'Yes. A mouse with light, responsive switches and a high polling rate registers rapid clicks more reliably than an old or heavy-switch mouse. Debounce time (a delay some mice add to filter accidental double-clicks) can also cap very fast clicking. Hardware matters, but technique and practice matter more.'
  - question: 'Can I use the spacebar instead of the mouse?'
    answer: 'Not on this page. This test counts mouse clicks only, so its scores stay comparable run to run. We have a separate spacebar speed test for keyboard pressing, with its own ranks and its own history. Pressing a key and clicking a mouse use different muscles and different hardware, which is why the two are scored apart.'
  - question: 'How can I click faster?'
    answer: 'Relax your hand, rest your wrist on the desk, and use short, controlled finger movements rather than pressing hard. Warm up first, keep a steady rhythm instead of a frantic burst, and use a lighter mouse. Consistent practice raises your baseline more than any single trick.'
  - question: 'Are autoclickers detected?'
    answer: "No. This test is for measuring your own click speed, so there's no autoclicker detection or anti-cheat. Your results live only in your browser, so an autoclicker only cheats your own records."
  - question: 'How is my data stored?'
    answer: "Your browser stores everything (best CPS, run history, trend, achieved ranks, and settings) locally using localStorage. Nothing reaches any server, so your results stay private on your device and won't sync across browsers."

  <div class="container">
    <h2>What is a clicks per second test?</h2>

      A clicks per second test, or CPS test, measures how many times you can click your mouse in a
      fixed window of time. Your score is total clicks divided by the test duration: your clicks per
      second.


      Click speed matters in games that reward rapid inputs, from Minecraft PvP bridging and combos
      to idle and clicker games. It's also a fun, low-stakes way to benchmark your hand speed and
      endurance, and to see whether a new mouse or technique makes a difference.


      This test counts mouse clicks only. To measure keyboard speed instead, use the{' '}
      <a href="/spacebar-speed-test">spacebar speed test</a>, which keeps its own ranks and history.

      The test also pairs well with a <a href="/">reaction time test</a>: one measures how fast you
      respond, the other how fast you sustain inputs.

<section id="factors" class="info-section alt-bg">
  <div class="container">
    <h2>Factors that affect your clicks per second</h2>
    <div class="factors-list section-content">
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Technique</h3>

          Normal clicking tops out around 6–8 CPS for most people. Jitter clicking (a controlled arm
          vibration) reaches 10–14, and butterfly clicking (two alternating fingers) can hit 15–25.
          Technique is the single biggest factor.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Mouse hardware</h3>

          Switch weight, polling rate, and debounce time all shape how fast clicks register. A
          gaming mouse with light switches and low debounce will out-click an old office mouse.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Duration & fatigue</h3>

          A 5-second burst flatters your peak speed. Over 30 or 60 seconds your muscles fatigue and
          your CPS drops, which is why endurance runs are ranked as a bigger achievement.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Hand and arm position</h3>

          A supported wrist, a relaxed grip, and a comfortable desk height let you click faster for
          longer. Cramped or tense positions cost you clicks and invite strain.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Warm-up & focus</h3>

          Cold starts run slow. A short warm-up and a distraction-free environment can add a full
          click per second or more to your score.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Grip and button</h3>

          Claw and fingertip grips put your finger closer to the switch than a palm grip, which
          shortens every click. The left and right buttons can also differ on the same mouse, since
          switch wear is rarely even. Our{' '}
          <a href="/right-click-cps-test">right click CPS test</a> measures that difference.

      </div>
    </div>

  </div>
</section>
