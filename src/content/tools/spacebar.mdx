Pick a duration, press the spacebar as fast as you can, and your score is your total presses divided by the seconds elapsed.     answer: 'Yes. Linear mechanical switches actuate partway down and reset fast, so they register quick repeated taps better than a membrane keyboard that needs a full press. <section id="what-is" class="info-section">
  <div class="container">
    <h2>What is a spacebar speed test?</h2>

      A spacebar speed test measures how many times you can press the spacebar in a fixed window of
      time. Choose 5, 10, 30, or 60 seconds, then tap the bar as fast as your hand allows. Your
      score is your total presses divided by the duration: your presses per second.

      The spacebar is the key games lean on hardest. Minecraft players mash it to jump and bridge,
      runners and platformers map it to a sprint, and half the browser games ever made turn it into
      the only control you have. Knowing how fast yours goes tells you whether the bottleneck is
      your hand or your keyboard.

      This test only accepts the spacebar. If you want to measure mouse clicks instead, use the
      <a href="/cps">CPS test</a>.

  </div>
</section>

<section id="how-it-works" class="info-section alt-bg">
  <div class="container">
    <h2>How this spacebar speed test works</h2>
    <ol class="steps-list section-content">
      <li>
        <strong>Pick a duration</strong>

          Choose 5, 10, 30, or 60 seconds. Your browser remembers the choice for next time.

      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Press space to start</strong>

          Your first press starts the clock and counts as press number one. Nothing to click, no
          countdown to wait through. On a phone or tablet, tap the key on screen instead.

      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Keep pressing</strong>

          The readout shows your live presses per second, your press count, and the seconds left.
          The bar under the test area drains as your time runs down.

      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Read your score</strong>

          When time is up the test locks and shows your final score, your total presses, and a rank
          from Beginner to Elite. Beat your record and the "New best!" flag appears.

      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Track your progress</strong>

          Your best run, average, trend graph, achieved ranks, and history all save in your browser
          so you can watch the number climb.

      </li>
    </ol>
    <div class="note">
      <strong>Note:</strong> Holding the spacebar down does nothing. Your operating system fires
      auto-repeat events when a key is held, and the test throws all of them away. One press, one
      count.
    </div>

  </div>
</section>

<section id="tips" class="info-section">
  <div class="container">
    <h2>How to press the spacebar faster</h2>
    <div class="tips-grid section-content">
      <div class="tip-card">
        <span class="tip-card-icon" aria-hidden="true">
          ✌️
        </span>
        <h3>Alternate two fingers</h3>

          One thumb tops out around 7 presses per second. Two index fingers taking turns on the bar
          get most people past 10 within a few attempts. This is the single biggest change you can
          make.

      </div>
      <div class="tip-card">
        <span class="tip-card-icon" aria-hidden="true">
          ✋
        </span>
        <h3>Rest your palms</h3>

          Plant both palms on the desk and let your fingers do the travel. Holding your hands in the
          air burns energy you want going into the key.

      </div>
      <div class="tip-card">
        <span class="tip-card-icon" aria-hidden="true">
          🎯
        </span>
        <h3>Hit the centre</h3>

          The spacebar is the longest key on the board and rides on stabilisers. The middle presses
          cleanest, while the far edges tilt and cost you actuations.

      </div>
      <div class="tip-card">
        <span class="tip-card-icon" aria-hidden="true">
          🎵
        </span>
        <h3>Find a rhythm</h3>

          A cadence you can hold beats a frantic opening that fades at second four. Longer durations
          punish the sprint start hardest.

      </div>
      <div class="tip-card">
        <span class="tip-card-icon" aria-hidden="true">
          🪶
        </span>
        <h3>Tap, do not push</h3>

          Most switches actuate around halfway down. Bottoming out the key wastes travel and slows
          the reset, so aim for light taps that just clear the actuation point.

      </div>
      <div class="tip-card">
        <span class="tip-card-icon" aria-hidden="true">
          🔥
        </span>
        <h3>Warm up first</h3>

          Cold hands run slow. Two throwaway runs before your real attempt are worth most of a press
          per second.

      </div>
    </div>

  </div>
</section>

<section id="factors" class="info-section alt-bg">
  <div class="container">
    <h2>Factors that affect your spacebar speed</h2>
    <div class="factors-list section-content">
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Technique</h3>

          One thumb reaches 6 to 8 presses per second for most people. Alternating two fingers
          roughly doubles the ceiling, and drumming three fingers along the bar goes higher again at
          the cost of consistency.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Switch type</h3>

          Linear mechanical switches actuate partway through the travel and reset quickly, which
          suits rapid taps. Membrane and scissor keyboards need a fuller press and a fuller release,
          so the same hand scores lower on a laptop than on a mechanical board.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Debounce time</h3>

          Keyboards ignore a second signal that arrives within a few milliseconds of the first,
          which is how they filter electrical noise from a single press. On a board with a long
          debounce window, very fast tapping loses presses that your fingers made.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Stabilisers</h3>

          The spacebar spans several key units and needs metal bars underneath to keep it level.
          Worn or rattly stabilisers make the key press unevenly, which shows up as dropped
          actuations near the ends.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Duration and fatigue</h3>

          Five seconds measures your peak. Thirty or sixty seconds measures your forearm, and almost
          everyone gives up a press or two per second by the end of a long run.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Polling rate</h3>

          A keyboard reports its state to your computer on a fixed interval, often every 8
          milliseconds at 125Hz. Two presses inside a single interval can arrive as one event, which
          caps the top end regardless of how fast your fingers move.

      </div>
    </div>

  </div>
</section>
