<section id="what-is" class="info-section">
  <div class="container">
    <h2>What is a right click CPS test?</h2>

      A right click CPS test measures how many times you can press your right mouse button per
      second. Pick a duration, right click as fast as you can, and your score is total clicks
      divided by the time. Only right clicks count. Left clicks do nothing, and the test area
      blocks the context menu so it never interrupts a run.

      Most click tests only measure your index finger. Your right click finger does a fraction of
      that work day to day, which is why right click scores run lower and why the number is worth
      training on its own: games like Minecraft lean on right click for blocking and block
      placement, and MOBAs issue every move command with it.

      For the standard left-button version, use our <a href="/cps">CPS test</a>. For the fixed
      10-second classic, take the <a href="/kohi-click-test">Kohi click test</a>.

</section>

  <div class="container">
    <h2>How this right click test works</h2>
    <ol class="steps-list section-content">
      <li>
        <strong>Pick a duration</strong>

          Choose 5, 10, 30, or 60 seconds. Your choice is saved for next time. Five seconds shows
          your peak speed; the longer runs test how well you hold it.

      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Right click to start</strong>

          Your first right click starts the timer and counts as click number one. No countdown, no
          ready screen, and no context menu.

      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Keep clicking</strong>

          Right click inside the test area as fast as you can. The live readout shows your CPS,
          your click count, and the seconds left, and the bar below the area drains as time runs
          out.

      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Read your score</strong>

          When time's up, the test locks and shows your final CPS, total clicks, and a rank from
          Beginner to Elite. Beat your best and the "New best!" flag appears.

      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Track your progress</strong>

          Your best CPS, average, trend graph, achieved ranks, and run history save in your
          browser, so you can watch the weaker finger catch up.

      </li>
    </ol>
    <div class="note">
      <strong>Note:</strong> Left clicks don't score. If nothing happens when you click, check that
      you're using the right button, and on a trackpad use a two-finger click.
    </div>

<section id="why-slower" class="info-section alt-bg">
  <div class="container">
    <h2>Why right click is slower than left click</h2>

      Your index finger clicks thousands of times a day: every link, every file, every game input.
      The finger on your right button does a fraction of that, so it starts with less speed and
      less endurance. Testing both buttons usually shows a gap of 1 to 2 CPS.

      Grip closes part of the gap. A claw or fingertip grip arches the fingers and gives the ring
      finger a clean vertical tap. A flat palm grip stretches it across the button at an angle,
      which costs speed. Hardware plays a part too: right switches on some mice are heavier or
      mushier than the left, and switch weight you'd never notice in normal use shows up at speed.

      The good news: an untrained finger improves fast. Short practice sessions over a week or two
      raise most people's right click CPS by a full click per second.

  </div>
</section>

<section id="tips" class="info-section alt-bg">
  <div class="container">
    <h2>How to right click faster</h2>
    <div class="factors-list section-content">
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Fix your grip first</h3>

          Claw grip arches your fingers so the ring finger taps straight down. If you palm the
          mouse, try lifting your knuckles; most people gain speed within a few runs.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Tap, don't press</h3>

          Click from the knuckle with a light, quick tap and let the switch reset. Pressing through
          the button wastes motion and tires the finger.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Anchor your wrist</h3>

          Rest your wrist on the desk and keep the hand relaxed. Tension is the main thing that
          makes a right click run fade in the second half.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Hold a rhythm</h3>

          A cadence you can keep for the whole run beats a frantic start. This matters more on the
          30 and 60-second durations, where the untrained finger fades fast.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Check your hardware</h3>

          Some mice fit heavier or mushier switches on the right button. If your right click feels
          like more work than your left on the same mouse, the switch is part of your score.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Practice in short sessions</h3>

          A few runs a day beats one long grind, and an untrained finger improves fast. Stop if
          anything hurts; a strained finger loses more training days than a rest does.

      </div>
    </div>
