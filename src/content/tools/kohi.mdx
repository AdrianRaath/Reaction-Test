<section id="what-is" class="info-section">
  <div class="container">
    <h2>What is the Kohi click test?</h2>

      The Kohi click test is a 10-second click speed test named after Kohi, a Minecraft Hardcore
      Factions server that hosted one on its website. The server shut down years ago. Players kept
      using the format anyway, and it became the standard benchmark for click speed in the PvP
      community.

      The format is strict: click as fast as you can for 10 seconds, and your score is your total
      clicks divided by ten. Ten seconds is long enough to expose a fading hand, so the test rewards
      a pace you can hold rather than a two-second burst.

      Want to pick your own duration? Use our <a href="/cps">CPS test</a>. Want to measure the
      keyboard instead? That is the <a href="/spacebar-speed-test">spacebar speed test</a>. This
      page keeps the fixed Kohi format.

  </div>
</section>

<section id="kohi-vs-cps" class="info-section alt-bg">
  <div class="container">
    <h2>Kohi click test vs CPS test</h2>

      Both tests measure clicks per second with a mouse. The difference is the format. The Kohi
      test is fixed: 10 seconds, the same conditions every run. Our <a href="/cps">CPS test</a> lets
      you pick a duration from 5 to 60 seconds.

      The fixed format is the point. Because every Kohi run is identical, your scores stay
      comparable across weeks of practice, and you can judge a new mouse or a new technique against
      a stable baseline.

      Run both if you like: sprint the 5-second CPS test for peak speed, then hold the 10-second
      Kohi test for endurance. A <a href="/">reaction time test</a> completes the picture by
      measuring how fast you respond in the first place.

  </div>
</section>

<section id="techniques" class="info-section">
  <div class="container">
    <h2>Jitter, butterfly, and drag clicking</h2>

      Click speed above 8 CPS usually comes from technique rather than raw finger speed. These are
      the methods the PvP community developed, from safest to most extreme:

    <div class="factors-list section-content">
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Normal clicking</h3>

          One finger, one button. Tops out around 6 to 8 CPS for most hands. It gives you the best
          aim and the least strain, which is why most players stick with it.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Jitter clicking</h3>

          Tense your forearm so your hand vibrates on the mouse. Reaches 10 to 14 CPS with
          practice. Aiming while jittering is hard, and long sessions are rough on the wrist.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Butterfly clicking</h3>

          Alternate two fingers on one button. Can reach 15 to 25 CPS. Some servers ban it because
          it can exploit double-click behaviour on certain mice.

      </div>
      <div class="factor">
        <h3>Drag clicking</h3>

          Drag a finger across a textured button so friction fires a burst of clicks, sometimes 30+
          CPS. It only works on certain mice, and most servers treat it as cheating.

      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="note">
      <strong>Note:</strong> This test counts every click your mouse registers, whatever the
      technique. Ten seconds of jitter or butterfly clicking strains the hand, so stop if anything
      hurts.
    </div>

  </div>
</section>
